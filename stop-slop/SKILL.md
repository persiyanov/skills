# Stop Slop

Eliminate predictable AI writing patterns from prose.

## Core Rules

1. **Cut filler words.** Remove throat-clearing openers, emphasis crutches, banned AI vocabulary, and empty adverbs. Any other adverb must change the sentence's meaning when deleted, or it goes too. See [references/phrases.md](references/phrases.md).

2. **Break formulaic structures.** Avoid binary contrasts, negative listings, dramatic fragmentation, rhetorical setups, false agency, colon reveals, fake-analysis clauses, synonym cycling, fake-strong verbs. See [references/structures.md](references/structures.md).

3. **Use active voice.** Every sentence needs a human subject doing something. No passive constructions. No inanimate objects performing human actions ("the complaint becomes a fix").

4. **Be specific.** No vague declaratives ("The reasons are structural"). Name the specific thing. No lazy extremes ("every," "always," "never") doing vague work. No importance puffery ("marks a pivotal moment"): state the fact and let the reader judge. No unsourced authority ("experts agree," "studies show"): name the source or cut the claim.

5. **Put the reader in the room.** No narrator-from-a-distance voice. "You" beats "People." Specifics beat abstractions.

6. **Vary rhythm.** Mix sentence lengths. Two items beat three. End paragraphs differently. No em dashes. No semicolons. Split into two sentences instead.

7. **Trust readers.** State facts directly. Skip softening, justification, hand-holding, and lone-expert flattery ("what most people miss").

8. **End on substance.** If it sounds like a pull-quote or a mic-drop, delete it. Don't rewrite it into a better one. Don't preserve its rhythm. No recap endings ("In conclusion," a final paragraph restating the piece). End on the last concrete point, takeaway, or next action already in the draft.

9. **Format follows content.** No emoji in headings. No bold sprinkled mid-sentence for emphasis. No bullet list where two sentences of prose read better. No header over a two-sentence section.

## Quick Checks

Before delivering prose:

- Adverb from the banned list? Kill it. Any other adverb: delete it, and if the meaning holds, it stays deleted.
- Any passive voice? Find the actor, make them the subject.
- Inanimate thing doing a human verb ("the decision emerges")? Name the person.
- Sentence starts with a Wh- word? Restructure it.
- Any "here's what/this/that" throat-clearing? Cut to the point.
- Any "not X, it's Y" contrasts? State Y directly.
- Trailing "-ing" clause pretending to explain ("highlighting," "underscoring")? Cut it or state the real consequence.
- Colon reveal ("The best part: it learns")? Rewrite as a plain sentence.
- "Experts agree" / "studies show"? Name the source or cut the claim.
- Puffery ("stands as a testament," "plays a vital role")? State the bare fact.
- Same thing named three ways (agent/assistant/tool)? Repeat the clear word.
- "Serves as" / "acts as"? Say "is," or say what it actually does.
- Faux-insight setup ("the part everyone misses")? Cut the setup, keep the claim.
- Three consecutive sentences match length? Break one.
- Paragraph ends with punchy one-liner? Vary it.
- Ending restates the piece or lands a metaphor? Delete it. End on the last concrete point.
- Emoji heading, decorative bold, bullets that should be prose? Strip them.
- Em-dash or semicolon anywhere? Remove it. Split the sentence or use a period.
- Vague declarative ("The implications are significant")? Name the specific implication.
- Narrator-from-a-distance ("Nobody designed this")? Put the reader in the scene.
- Meta-joiners ("The rest of this essay...")? Delete. Let the essay move.
