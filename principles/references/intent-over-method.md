# Intent over Method

When you ask someone to do work, specify it, or judge it, tell them what must be true when it's done, why, and how anyone can tell. Leave the method to them. People who know the purpose adapt when the plan meets reality and handle the cases you didn't foresee. People who have only steps follow them past the point where the steps stop making sense (U.S. Army, ADP 6-0, "Mission Command," 2019).

State three things:

1. The outcome: what must be true when the work is done.
2. The reason: what fails, and for whom, without it. The reason lets them handle a case that you didn't name.
3. The test: how anyone can tell that the outcome holds. If no result could fail the test, it's a slogan, not an intent.

Specify the method only in two cases:

- A convention that must stay the same every time, such as a format that other tools read, a naming scheme, or a layout that people have learned.
- A fact that they can't work out, such as a tool's limit, a past decision, or a constraint from outside.

The same split applies to every kind of handoff:

| Handoff | The intent to give | The method that creeps in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delegating to a person or an agent | The outcome, the reason, and the test | Lists of steps, formats, and tools |
| A spec or a ticket | The guarantees, and how to verify them | The mechanism that provides them |
| A design brief | The people, their job, and what good looks like | The layout |
| Feedback | The problem, and why it matters | The fix |
| A rule or a guideline | What must hold, and why | A checklist of cases |

When the result misses, ask whether they lacked the outcome, the reason, or the test, and fix that before you add a step. A step added for each miss makes the instructions longer, and the next miss is one that no step named. If the same miss comes back after the intent is clear, change the structure instead: [Encode Lessons in Structure](encode-lessons-in-structure.md).

Ask: Does whoever does the work know what must be true, why, and how to tell, and is each step I gave them a convention or a fact that they couldn't work out?
