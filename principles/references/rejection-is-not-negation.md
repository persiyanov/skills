# Rejection Is Not Negation

A claim has three states: asserted, denied, or not made. A rule can require, forbid, or say
nothing. A fact can be true, false, or unknown. A field can be true, false, or missing. Thinking
in two values drops the third state.

Rejecting a claim returns it to not made. Its opposite is a new claim and needs its own evidence.
"Always" and "never" can both be false. Denying "must list titles" leaves "may skip titles", and
"must not list titles" is a third claim.

Make a claim only as strong as its evidence. With no evidence, make none.

In a review or an audit, every item can stay, flip, or go. Ask whether it should exist before
asking whether it is right.

Sources: Aristotle's square of opposition (contraries can both be false). Deontic logic's three
statuses (obligatory, permitted, forbidden). Hume: "A wise man proportions his belief to the
evidence."

Ask: Did anyone assert the opposite, or only reject the claim?
