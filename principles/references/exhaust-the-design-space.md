# Exhaust the Design Space

Before you commit to an important design choice, compare options that differ in substance, because the first plausible answer may not be the best one.

Name what could change the choice, and settle it with existing evidence, sketches, prototypes, or measurements. Cosmetic variations of one design aren't real alternatives. For example, for an "undo send" feature, a delay before sending, a recall request, and an edit window are three options. Three styles of the same toast are one option.

This helps most with unfamiliar interactions, architectural choices, and product behavior that people must try before they understand it. An established pattern, a clear fix, or constraints that leave one workable option usually need no further exploration.

Stop when you have enough evidence to choose, and then carry the choice through. Exploring is your work, not the reader's: bring the chosen option, fully worked through, and record why each alternative lost. A list with no choice hands your thinking to someone else.

Ask: Which options that differ in substance did I compare, and what fact decided the choice?
