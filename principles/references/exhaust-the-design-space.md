# Exhaust the Design Space

Compare genuinely different options before committing to an important design choice. The first plausible answer may not be the best one.

Name what could change the choice. Use existing evidence, sketches, prototypes, or measurements to settle it. Cosmetic variations of one design are not real alternatives.

This helps with unfamiliar interactions, architectural choices, and product behavior that needs to be tried to be understood. An established pattern, a clear fix, or constraints that leave only one workable option usually need no further exploration.

Stop when you have enough evidence to choose. Then carry the choice through. Exploring is your work, not the reader's: bring the chosen option, fully worked through, and record why each alternative lost. A list with no choice hands your thinking to someone else.

Ask: Which genuinely different options did I compare, and what fact decided it?
